# HiringSignals.ai — Content & Positioning Sprint

## Context for this sprint

HiringSignals.ai is being repositioned from a standalone recruiting consultancy into the first domain-specific deployment of the iDIG protocol — a live proof case for using Hilbert Space vector alignment to reduce noise in the hiring funnel. The owner, J. Paul Duplantis, is an active recruiter who works inside the problem daily. That operational reality is a feature, not a liability.

The sister properties are:

- **i-DIG.io** — the protocol, the framework, the mothership
- **The Resonant Builders** (blog/podcast on i-DIG.io) — the community conversation around the protocol
- **iDIG Movies** — the second domain proof case (narrative resonance)

HiringSignals.ai and iDIG Movies are framed as domains — real-world environments where the same underlying math is being tested against real problems with real stakes.

---

## The Core Repositioning

**Away from:** "I am a recruiting consultant who uses smart tools"

**Toward:** "Hiring is the first domain where I am proving that signal-driven alignment outperforms keyword matching — and I am doing it from inside the work, not from the outside looking in"

The key tension to name on the site: traditional hiring tools (ATS systems, keyword filters, LinkedIn boolean search) are built to match amplitude — volume, frequency, surface similarity. HiringSignals is built around magnitude and direction — what the candidate is actually pointing toward, and whether that vector aligns with where the role and organization are heading.

## Speculative Content Sections to Consider

### 1. The Problem Statement
Name the noise clearly. Recruiters and hiring managers are drowning in applicants who look right on paper and aren't. The tools built to help — ATS ranking, keyword filters, skills matching — were designed to reduce volume, not surface resonance. They flatten the signal.

### 2. The HiringSignals Approach
Frame the methodology without over-engineering the language. Something like:

- Listen for direction, not just description — what is this person moving toward, not just what have they done
- Read the role as a vector, not a checklist — what does this team or organization need to move forward, not just what skills does it list
- Surface alignment, not similarity — two candidates can look identical on a resume and be pointing in completely different directions

### 3. The Lab Connection
A short section that connects HiringSignals explicitly to the iDIG protocol — honest about the fact that this is a live testing ground. Something like: "Every search I run is also a data point. HiringSignals is where the iDIG protocol meets the hiring funnel — and where I learn what signal-driven alignment actually looks like in practice."

This is honest, it's interesting, and it differentiates from every other recruiting site on the internet.

**Theoretical anchor — Alexander Wendt:**
The intellectual foundation sits in Alexander Wendt's *Quantum Mind and Social Science* — the argument that human consciousness and social behavior cannot be accurately modeled using classical mechanics. Traditional hiring tools do exactly that: they treat candidates as classical particles with fixed, measurable attributes. The iDIG protocol applies quantum social science to the hiring funnel — using Hilbert Space vector math (the same mathematical structure Wendt uses to model social states) to surface direction and alignment rather than surface-level similarity.

This reference belongs one layer beneath the home page — a short callout in The Lab Connection section, linking out to The Resonant Builders post where the full argument is developed. Keep the home page in the practitioner voice. Let the theory live in the blog.

> *Reference: Alexander Wendt, Quantum Mind and Social Science (2015) — [The Awakening blog](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WpkhPgpY28M)*

### 4. The Resonant Builders Link
A subtle but clear connection — Paul writes about the hiring signal problem in The Resonant Builders. This section invites visitors into that conversation. Not a sales pitch — an invitation to think alongside the build.

### 5. Work With Me
Keep this simple and direct. Paul recruits. If you need a recruiter who thinks about signal differently, here's how to start a conversation. Form posts to paul@hiringsignals.ai.
